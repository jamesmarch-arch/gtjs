# gtjs — design

A browser factory game: GregTech: New Horizons progression, simplified, drawn in the
Colorfigures style (hand-inked, flat-coloured, isometric, every machine works when you open it).

## Pillars

1. **GT's progression is the game.** Voltage tiers, recipe chains, ore processing and the
   first multiblock. These are what make GTNH feel like GTNH; keep them.
2. **Every machine is a figure.** Opening a machine shows a Colorfigure you can turn, see through
   and operate: the macerator grinds, the boiler's firebox glows, the EBF coils heat. It is the
   machine's GUI.
3. **Simple where GTNH is tedious.** No player avatar, no mining world, no crafting grid, no pollution.
   Items move through one shared network instead of pipes and conveyors.

## Shape of the game

- **The floor:** an isometric grid you build on. Machines are 1×1 tiles, multiblocks are larger.
  The floor grows as you progress.
- **Items and fluids:** one shared storage network (early: "Storage crate"; later: an ME-style
  upgrade). Machines that are set to a recipe pull their inputs from it and push their outputs back.
- **Steam:** boilers and steam machines connect through **pipe** tiles. Each connected pipe group
  shares its steam.
- **Power (EU):** generators, batteries and machines connect through **cable** tiles. Each connected
  cable group is one power net.
  - A net runs at the voltage of its highest-tier generator.
  - A machine on a net with a higher voltage than its own **explodes** (GT's classic lesson), unless
    it is fed through a transformer.
  - Cables have a voltage limit and burn out above it.
- **Ore:** there is no map to mine. Cobblestone is the only thing gathered by hand, and everything else is crushed and
  sifted out of it (see *Everything from cobblestone*).
- **Quest book:** a tiered list of goals that teaches the chains and gates what comes next, as in GTNH.

## First playable slice: Steam age → LV

| Stage | Key machines | Key chain | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Crusher, sifter, furnace, soaking barrel, tree farm, coke oven (multiblock, 3×3) | cobblestone → gravel → sifted coal and ore; coal → coke + creosote | First bronze |
| Steam | Bronze boiler, steam macerator / furnace / compressor / alloy smelter / forge hammer / extractor | 3 copper + 1 tin → 4 bronze; ore → crushed → impure dust → ingot | Steel (bricked blast furnace) |
| LV | Steam turbine, battery buffer, tin cable, LV macerator / furnace / ore washer / wiremill / bender / lathe / assembler / centrifuge / extractor | rubber, wires, vacuum tube → basic circuit; ore washer byproducts | Build an **Electric Blast Furnace** |

The slice ends when the EBF makes its first aluminium ingot: MV is unlocked (out of scope for now).

### GT mechanics we keep

- Tiers: ULV 8, LV 32, MV 128 EU/t.
- Recipes have EU/t (or steam/t), a duration in ticks, inputs, outputs and chanced outputs.
- Overclocking: one tier above a recipe means 2× speed for 4× power (GTNH rules).
- Steam machines run at half the speed of their LV counterparts and never overclock.
- Explosions from over-voltage; cables burn out.

### Deliberately left out

Pollution, cable loss, machine sides and facing, item pipes and conveyors, hand crafting with tools, the player world.

## Architecture

```
src/
  sim/        pure TypeScript game logic, no DOM. 20 ticks/s (like Minecraft).
    data/     items, recipes, machines, quests: plain data, no code
    world.ts  floors (the factory floor and compact floors' inner floors), placed machines, storage
    nets.ts   steam nets (connected components of pipes, across floors through ports); power.ts: EU nets
    autocraft.ts  the crafting planner; jobs.ts: crafting jobs that carry a plan out on machines
    tick.ts   recipe processing, quest progress
    save.ts   localStorage + JSON export/import
  view/
    floor/    isometric floor renderer (Canvas2D, figure sprites, paper background)
    figure/   machine close-up: hosts a figure page in an iframe, bridge messages
    ui/       storage panel, quest book, build menu
public/
  figures/
    core.js           Colorfigures engine (MIT, unmodified copy), shared by every figure
    cf-head.js        the skill build script's page extras (zoom, notices, phone layout), shared;
    cf-tail.js        generated by `npm run figures:extras`
    gt-bridge.js      connects a figure page to the game (protocol documented at its top)
    <machine>.html    one figure per machine, authored with the Colorfigures skill
  sprites/            floor sprites: <machine>.png + .json (tile corners), made by `npm run sprites`
tools/
  extract_cf_page.py    writes cf-head.js / cf-tail.js from the skill's scripts/build.py
  snapshot-figures.mjs  headless Chrome renders each figure at the default view into a floor sprite
```

A figure page loads `cf-head.js`, `core.js`, `gt-bridge.js`, `gt-parts.js`, its own script, then `cf-tail.js`. It
needs no build step: the skill's `build.py` (which inlines the engine into each page) is not used.

`gt-parts.js` holds what machine figures share: materials and the bronze palette, gauge, lamp, steam riser with its
valve, vent stack, puffs, hoppers, ingots, the induction-crucible kit (turbine, dynamo, coil, tilting crucible), and
`gtMachine()`, which mounts a figure and wires it to the game (or runs a demo when the page is opened on its own).
A new machine figure is its geometry plus one `gtMachine({...})` call. The steam macerator predates it and still has
its own copies.

**The LV look.** `gt-lv.js` (loaded after `gt-parts.js`) gives every LV machine one silver steel case: a dark band
across the top of the front with the blue LV badge, the name plate and the lamp; the energy socket and its cable on
the left side; louvres on the right; a vent grille on the roof; an output slot and tray. Machines are told apart by
their **face**, the recess in the middle of the front, where each shows its own work (the macerator's crusher
rollers, the washer's porthole, the bender's ram), and by what they carry on the roof. An LV figure is `gtLv({...})`
with `face`, `parts`, `inside` and `product`. Generators, batteries and transformers pass `noRecipe: true`: they
have only a power switch and read `rate` (0..1 of the EU they make or move), `energy` and `mode` from the game.

The stone crusher, stone sifter, soaking barrel and tree farm are built in cobblestone and wood on the furnace's model;
the steam crusher and sifter have the steam family's bronze; the LV crusher and sifter each have a face of their own.

Every machine has a figure. Screenshot URLs take the
skill's `?yaw= &pitch= &xr=1 &labels=1` plus `run=1` (working), `freeze=1` (hold one still frame: headless Chrome
can't keep up with a running loop), `progress=`, `heat=`, `vent=1`, `puffs=1`.

Two sorting lessons (core.js has no depth buffer): draw feet before everything, never in the same sort as the body,
and sort parts standing on the roof in their own pass after the body. Parts separated along two axes with opposite
answers (a foot vs the vent stack, the chimney vs the steam outlet) make the sort go round in a circle.

The simulation never touches the DOM; the view reads sim state each frame. Data in `sim/data` is
the only thing content work should need to change.

## Art pipeline (the Colorfigures skill)

The skill lives in `.claude/skills/colorfigures`. For each machine:

1. **Author** `public/figures/<machine>.html` following the skill's SKILL.md (truth first,
   parts list, see-through, labels, verify from every angle). The figure loads the shared
   `core.js` instead of inlining it, so one copy of the engine is shared.
2. **Bridge it.** The figure includes `gt-bridge.js` and keeps its state in sync with the game:
   - game → figure: `{ type:'state', running, progress, recipe, inputs, outputs, tier, power }`
   - figure → game: `{ type:'setRecipe', id }`, `{ type:'toggle' }`
   Figure buttons become machine controls: start/stop, a recipe picker. The animations
   (grinding, glowing, steam) are driven by `running` and `progress` from the sim, not by timers.
3. **Snapshot** the closed figure at the default view (and a running frame) into floor sprites
   with `tools/snapshot-figures`.
4. **Look rules for GT:** steam-age machines use bronze (a new palette: `{l:'#e8a85c', m:'#c4843c', d:'#4f3115'}`),
   LV machines use steel grey with the tier colour as an accent band, and every machine
   shows its tier on a small plate.

Credit: Colorfigures, ISO-GLOW and iso-figure are MIT. Keep `public/figures/LICENSE` and
credit them on the game's About screen. Figures may set `credit:false` inside the game.

## Milestones

- **M0** Design doc, project scaffold, skill installed. Done.
- **M1** Art pipeline proof: the **steam macerator** as a figure, bridged to a stub sim, plus its floor sprite. Done.
  Known limits: the sim runs on `requestAnimationFrame`, so it pauses in a background tab; headless Chrome cannot
  render the running see-through state under `--virtual-time-budget` (verify that state in a real browser).
- **M2** Sim core: items, recipes, boiler, steam macerator, steam furnace, storage, save/load, unit tests. Done:
  machines and pipes sit on a floor grid; touching pipes and steam machines form a steam net that shares one tank
  (each member holds a share in proportion to its capacity, so splits and merges keep the steam where it was).
  Saves are versioned JSON in localStorage (autosave every 10 s and on leaving), with export/import; loading
  re-places everything through the normal placement rules and pauses autosave if a save can't be read.
  The steam furnace and alloy smelter run in the sim but show a plain panel until they get figures.
  Known limit: two open tabs both autosave, so the last one to save wins.
- **M3** Floor: place and remove machines, pipes and cables, isometric rendering with sprites. Done:
  a Canvas2D floor (`src/view/floor/floor.ts`) draws figure sprites where a machine has one and a plain inked box
  in the same palette where it doesn't; pipes and cables join their neighbours. Tools: open (a machine's figure or
  panel opens as a GUI over the floor), remove (drag; refunds the cost), pipe and cable (drag to lay), and one per
  machine. Building costs items from storage (placeholder costs until M4 crafting). Zoom with the buttons or
  ctrl + wheel. Save format 2 adds cables; version 1 saves load unchanged. Cables are layout only until EU in M5.
- **M4** Steam age content complete and quest book. Done:
  - Machines and conduits are items: craft them at the workbench (`src/sim/craft.ts`, recipes in `crafts` in
    `src/sim/data/steam.ts`), place them to use one up, remove them to get it back. Hand crafting is instant and
    takes most of GT's hand tools as given (the hammer and mortar are real items, see Starting empty); by hand a plate costs 2 ingots, the forge hammer makes 2 from 3.
  - New steam machines: forge hammer (plates), extractor (sticky resin -> raw rubber), compressor (raw rubber ->
    rubber sheet). The coke oven is buildable from coke oven bricks (brick, sand, clay) and turns coal into coke,
    which burns twice as long. Clay, sand and sticky resin are gathered by hand (shift-click for 10).
  - Quest book (`src/sim/quests.ts`, data in `src/sim/data/quests.ts`): the Steam Age chapter, 14 quests from
    stoking the boilers to the first tin cables. Goals: made in total, held at once, built, fuel burned. Completed
    quests show a toast and a badge; rewards are claimed in the book.
  - Save format 3 adds stats and quest progress; earlier saves load with none.
  - The forge hammer, compressor, extractor and coke oven had no figures at first: plain boxes on the floor.
- **M5** LV: generators, EU nets, explosions, LV machines, the full ore processing chain, circuits. Done:
  - EU nets (`src/sim/power.ts`): cables and EU machines that touch share one buffer. A net runs at the voltage
    that feeds it each tick; a machine above its tier explodes (lost, with what it was working on) and a cable above
    its rating burns out. Tin cable 32 V, copper cable 128 V. A switched-on generator holding EU feeds its net even
    when the net is full and it makes nothing, so a full net loaded from a save still has its voltage.
  - Generators: LV steam turbine (2 L steam per EU, 32 EU/t; it sits on a pipe and a cable), LV semi-fluid
    generator (creosote, 8 EU/L). LV battery buffer (40,000 EU). High pressure coal boiler (15 L/t) for the steam.
  - LV/MV transformer: its high side is the tile in front (+x), its low side the other three. Step-down moves up to
    4 A x 32 V a tick from high to low; step-up moves it from low to high at 128 V. Step-up is the way to MV for
    the EBF in M6, and the way to blow up LV machines now.
  - LV machines overclock by GTNH's rule (`src/sim/tiers.ts`). The ore line: macerate (x2 crushed) -> wash
    (purified + byproduct) -> thermal centrifuge (centrifuged + byproduct) -> macerate (dust) -> smelt; the
    centrifuge cleans impure dust. Byproducts: iron -> nickel, copper -> gold, tin -> iron.
  - Circuits: glass -> glass tube -> vacuum tube; coal + copper wire -> resistor; wood planks + rubber -> coated
    board; all -> Electronic Circuit (assembler, or by hand for more). Wiremill and bender for wire and plates.
  - LV chapter of the quest book (15 quests), save format 4, steel-grey boxes for LV machines on the floor, power
    net list, explosions and burns shown on the floor and as toasts.
  - Simplifications: LV casings are iron (GTNH uses steel, which needs M6's blast furnace); the ore washer is
    plumbed to the water main like the boilers' feed; no cable loss or amperage limits except the transformer's.
- **M6** EBF multiblock and the end of the slice. Done:
  - The electric blast furnace (`src/sim/data/mv.ts`, figure `public/figures/ebf.html`): GT's 3x3x4 layout, with
    a casing layer (controller, input and output buses, MV energy hatch, maintenance hatch), two rings of eight
    cupronickel coils round a hollow core, and a casing layer with the muffler. It is assembled at the workbench from
    its parts and placed whole on a 3x3 footprint (not block by block). Coils give 1800 K; recipes need heat.
  - MV: an EU recipe runs only when its net's voltage covers its EU/t, so the EBF's 120 EU/t recipes need a net
    fed by a step-up transformer (with copper cable). On an LV net it stalls with "needs MV" and is not harmed.
  - Cupronickel (copper + the nickel byproduct) -> wire -> coils. Steel: iron + coal at 1000 K; LV casings now also
    have GTNH's steel recipe. Aluminium: bauxite -> dust -> centrifuge (GTNH uses an electrolyzer) -> EBF at 1700 K.
  - The quest book's last chapter ends with the first aluminium ingot; claiming it shows the end-of-slice screen.
  - The floor makes room above it for tall machines; multi-tile sprites render in a larger frame.

The slice is complete: steam age, LV and the EBF, from an empty floor to MV.

**Starting empty (after M6).** New games start with an empty floor and empty storage, as GTNH does. The bootstrap starts
with the one thing you can gather: cobblestone (shift-click for ten). Eight each makes a stone crusher, a stone sifter
and a coal-fired furnace (`stone_furnace`). Gravel from the crusher sifts to coal and ores. A stone hammer and a stone
mortar (cobblestone alone; `tools` on a recipe: needed in storage, never used up) hammer ore into crushed ore and grind
it to dust at the workbench; mix bronze dust (3 copper + 1 tin); smelt in the furnace (one coal smelts eight); then four
bronze plates and a furnace make the first boiler. It takes no bricks, so the barrel and the clay can come after it.
The quest book's opening follows that path: cobblestone, crusher, sifter, furnace, hand tools, bronze, boiler, soaking
barrel for clay and bricks, tree farm. It takes about three minutes of game time and 65 to 90 cobblestone from an empty
world to a boiler, the worst seed of ten under ten minutes (a test plays it). Tests (`src/sim/bootstrap.test.ts`) prove
that a boiler is reachable with hand crafting and unpowered machines alone, and every machine and item in the game from
cobblestone. The old starting kit lives on as a test fixture (`src/sim/fixtures.ts`).

**Everything from cobblestone.** After Ex Nihilo and skyblock packs: cobblestone is the only gatherable item
(`GATHERABLE`), and the data is in `src/sim/data/cobble.ts`. The more a block has been crushed, the later and rarer its
finds, so rarity pays for itself.
- *Crusher:* cobblestone → gravel → sand → stone dust, one for one, one stage a recipe.
- *Sifter:* one independent roll per find (the chanced outputs recipes already had), so a block can give several things
  or nothing. All its outputs are chanced, so the recipe has a `name` ("Sift Gravel") for the machine's list.

  | Block | Finds, each a chance per block |
  |---|---|
  | Gravel | Coal 30%, iron ore 30%, copper ore 30%, tin ore 20% |
  | Sand | Bauxite ore 15%, redstone 15%, lapis 8%, diamond 1% |
  | Stone dust | Nickel dust 15%, gold dust 8%, ender pearl 1% |
  | Dirt | Sapling 25% |

- *Tiers:* crusher and sifter each come three ways, each twice as fast as the one before, like the other machine
  families (`recipesForMachine` goes by kind and power): stone (cobblestone alone, no power; 4 s a crush, 6 s a sift),
  steam (4 L/t; 2 s, 3 s), LV (2 EU/t, which overclocks to 32 EU/t; 1 s, 1.5 s).
- *Soaking barrel:* no power; water comes from the main, as for the ore washer and boilers, so it is no item. Stone dust →
  clay, sand → dirt, 10 s each. Six cobblestone and two iron plates (its hoops; no wood, which comes after it).
- *Tree farm:* eight dirt and four cobblestone, no power. The recipe `grow_tree` has no inputs but `holds` a sapling: the
  machine takes one from storage when it first runs (`MachineState.holding`) and keeps it for good, giving it back when
  it is removed. Every 30 s: 2 wood planks, and a 50% chance of a sticky resin.
- *The gems' first uses:* redstone in the Electronic Circuit (one, by hand and in the assembler) and the battery buffer (4);
  lapis in the ME Terminal (4) and Crafting CPU (2); an ender pearl in the 9 x 9 compact floor; two pearls and two
  diamonds in the 13 x 13. Rooms bigger than their blocks take pearls, as in Compact Machines.
- *Old saves* (format 6 and before) load with everything they hold: things once gathered are just items. Quests they have
  done stay done, and the new ones at the start of the book stand open, to catch up on when they like.
- *To tune by playing.* The chances are a first pass. Gravel gives about 1.1 useful finds a block, so the opening is
  no grind; a pearl or a diamond (a hundred blocks on average) is meant to be a machine left running.

**The camera (after M6).** The floor is 32 x 32 tiles (`FLOOR` in `src/sim/world.ts`); saves from the 12 x 8 days
grow to it on load, with everything on its tile. The floor's frame is a fixed window with a camera: drag with the
middle button (or the left one with the open tool), or the arrows / WASD, to pan; the wheel zooms about the pointer;
hold the right button and drag sideways to turn the view freely, and let go to settle on the nearest quarter turn;
Q and E (or the turn buttons) swing a quarter turn; Home looks at the machines again. The camera's angle (in quarter
turns) maps the floor's (x, y) onto view axes (u, v) about the floor's middle (`rot` / `unrot` in `floor.ts`);
drawing, picking and the draw order all work in view space. Between quarter turns each machine shows the nearest
view's sprite upright, riding on the middle of its turning tile (bending it onto the turned tile shears it badly). Sprites are pictures, so `npm run sprites` renders every figure from four sides
(`<figure>.png`, `@1`, `@2`, `@3`, at yaw 45 + 90 per view), and a machine opened from a turned view opens its figure
from the same side.

**Sound.** `src/view/audio.ts` makes every sound in code with the Web Audio API, from one small kit (noise bursts
through a sliding filter, tones that fall or rise and die away), so there are no files to load and the sounds share a
character the way the figures do. Each play varies a little in pitch and level. So far: gathering by hand (stone
knocks; a shift-click of ten strikes three times; the kit still has the rings and crunches of the ores, coal, planks,
clay, sand and resin, from when those were gathered too), panels opening and closing, and the boilers: one looping hiss for all the lit
boilers (not one each), set every frame from where they are on screen (`Floor.hearing`: louder in the frame and
zoomed in, panned across it), with the safety valve's chuffing roar on top while a boiler's steam net is full. The speaker button in the toolbar (or M) mutes
it, remembered in the browser. Browsers allow sound only after a click, so the audio starts on the first click or
key; a hidden tab goes quiet.

**Item icons.** `src/view/ui/icons.ts` draws every item in code in the figures' style (flat light / mid / dark bands,
ink outline, the figures' palette). Most items are one of a few shapes coloured by what they are made of: ingot,
plate, dust heap (specked when impure), ore in the rock, broken ore (purified glints, centrifuged spins), wire spool,
cable coil, block (stone, brick, planks, glass, panel, coil), lump, drop, cut gem, heap of pebbles. Ore and what it breaks into take the
mineral's colour, not the metal's (rusty iron). About a dozen parts are drawn on their own (circuit, vacuum tube,
resistor, motor, the hand tools). Machines use their floor sprite, trimmed to the drawing in the browser. Each icon is
an SVG `<symbol>` defined once, and `icon(id)` returns a small `<svg><use>` for it, so long lists stay light. Icons
show in the workbench (output and inputs), the storage list and quest rewards; a test checks every item has one.

**Nested floors (compact floors).** After Compact Machines: a compact floor block is a 1 x 1 machine with a floor of
its own inside, which you step into and build on. Inner floors come in sized tiers (`src/sim/data/compact.ts`): 5 x 5
(steam age), 9 x 9 (LV), 13 x 13 (MV), each crafted from its tier's parts (see *Content* below). Blocks nest without
a limit: a compact floor can hold compact floors.
- *The world* (`src/sim/world.ts`). Every floor's layout is a `Level` (its size, machines, pipes, cables). The world is
  the factory floor's level and holds every inner floor in `w.floors`, by id (`f<n>`); a compact block's machine
  names its floor in `inner`, so each floor is owned by exactly one block. Layout functions (`placeMachine`, `build`,
  `demolish`, `canBuild`, `occupant`, ...) take the level to work on as their last argument, the factory floor when it
  is left out. `levels(w)` walks the floors depth first (each block's floor right after the floor it stands on), and
  every floor ticks in that order, so a game replays the same after a save. Storage, stats and quests stay the world's:
  every machine on every floor works from and into the one storage, so floors need no item ports.
- *Removing a block refunds it.* Its floor is emptied first: everything on it, and on the floors inside that, is
  demolished and goes back into storage as if removed by hand (unfinished recipes give back their inputs, steam and
  EU are lost), then the floor is gone. Items are counts in one storage, so a block can't carry its contents away.
- *Ports* sit on an inner floor's wall: the ring of tiles just off its edges (not the corners), where nothing else can
  go. A steam port or an EU port is free to place and to remove; it is part of the block. Pipes and cables join it from
  the tile inside. A block with a port of a kind becomes a link in the net of that kind round it outside, and every port
  of that kind inside it is the same link, so the net runs straight through the wall, at any depth (`floorLinks` in
  `src/sim/nets.ts`; `steamNets` and `euNets` build nets over all floors at once). Links hold nothing and carry any
  voltage, so one net is one net whichever floors it spans: an LV machine inside fed MV through a port explodes, a tin
  cable two floors down burns at 128 V. A block has no sides: pipes touching it on two sides join through it. A block
  with no port of a kind is not in that kind's net.
- *Saves* are format 5: `floors` holds each inner floor (machines, pipes, cables, ports) by id, written in tick order,
  and a block's machine carries `inner`. Loading places each floor's contents through the normal rules when it places
  the block that owns it; a block whose floor is missing, or two blocks naming one floor, is refused. Format 4 and
  older saves load as one floor, unchanged. Tests: `src/sim/floors.test.ts`.
- *The view* (`src/view/floor/floor.ts`) shows one floor at a time. Opening a compact block steps into its floor; a
  breadcrumb over the floor (Factory › Compact Floor (9×9) at (16, 16) › ...) steps back out, as does Backspace. Each
  floor keeps its own camera; one seen for the first time fits the frame, and a step eases the zoom in or out. An inner
  floor is drawn with a low wall round it and its ports in the wall (bronze for steam, steel with a yellow band for
  EU). The tools, the machine GUI, the hiss of boilers and explosions work on whichever floor is shown; the port tools
  show only on an inner floor. A breakage on another floor is a toast that says where. Compact blocks and ports are
  plain inked boxes until they get figures (the art task), and the figure test lets compact floors off until then.
- *Content* (`src/sim/data/compact.ts`). As in Compact Machines, a bigger room takes more and better wall, and each
  size costs what a few of its tier's machines do. Every block has a hull, walls, and glass for its window:
  - 5 x 5 (steam): a bronze hull, 3 dense bronze plates, 16 bricks, 4 glass, about 35 bronze plates in all: a steam
    machine's worth twice over, all of it within reach by hand and the coal furnace.
  - 9 x 9 (LV): an LV hull, 4 LV casings, 4 circuits, 8 glass: two LV machines' circuits and 40 iron plates.
  - 13 x 13 (MV): an LV hull, 4 dense steel plates, 8 circuits, 16 glass, 4 copper cable (MV cable in the walls).
    The 36 steel make it the blast furnace's, after the EBF.
  The wall part is the dense plate (`dense_bronze_plate`, `dense_steel_plate`): nine plates hammered into one at the
  workbench, or nine ingots in an LV bender (900 ticks), half the metal. Ports stay free: they are part of the block, and
  costing them would need the layout code (`build` / `demolish`) to charge and refund them, a follow-up if wanted.
  The bootstrap test checks the tiers: the 5x5 is reachable before steam, the 9x9 only with steam machines (rubber for
  the hull's cable), the 13x13 only with the blast furnace.
- *Quests* (the Compact Floors chapter, alongside the others; each size opens with its tier): dense plates (after the
  forge hammer) → build a 5x5 → *Through the wall*: a machine at work inside on steam from a steam port → build a
  9x9 (after the assembler) → *Power through the wall* (EU) and *Floors within floors* (one inside another) → build a
  13x13 (after steel). Two goal kinds serve them (`src/sim/quests.ts`): `inside` counts machines working a recipe at
  that moment on compact floors whose wall has a port of the machine's kind (steam or EU, or either), switched on and
  not stalled; `depth` is how deep the deepest compact floor is (1 on the factory floor, 2 inside that). `goalText(g)`
  says what a goal asks for, for the quest book.

**Autocrafting: the planner.** Crafting on demand, after Applied Energistics 2 and Refined Storage, but items stay in
the one storage: ask for 16 circuits and the game works out every hand craft and machine run it takes. The first piece
is the planner, `planCraft(target, count, from, data?)` in `src/sim/autocraft.ts`: pure, it reads `from.storage` and
the machines on the floor (`from.machines`; a World fits) and changes nothing. The terminal (below) asks it, and a
crafting job (further below) carries a plan out. It returns:

- `steps`, in order (run them top to bottom): a hand craft or a machine recipe, its runs, everything it uses and
  makes, the tools it needs, the machine that runs it and its time there (overclocked as it would run), and `depth` in
  the tree under the target, for indenting.
- `missing` raw materials to gather first, `blocked` (what can't be made now, and why), `fromStorage`, `leftovers`,
  the `tools` it makes, `byproducts` and `chanced` (expected), `totals` per recipe and per machine, `average` (some of
  it rests on chance), and `ok`.

The rules: take leftovers of earlier steps first, then storage, then make the rest; a step's runs round up and the
rest is a leftover the next steps use. Where something can be made for sure (a hand craft, or a recipe's guaranteed main
output), only those ways count: the extras of a recipe are a bonus, never planned on. Where it can only come by chance
(a sifter's finds, nickel and gold, sticky resin), any recipe with it as a chanced output is a way, planned on its
*average* yield: three iron ore at 30% is ten runs. Such a step is marked `average`; what else it finds, on average, goes
in the pool for later steps too (or the two finds of one sifter would each pay for all its runs, and bronze would take
half as much gravel again as it does), and a walk over the finished steps (`setGoals`) says in whole items what the plan
needs out of each average step (`until`). The same recipe on several tiers is one way, on the fastest machine built (with
none built, the tier to build first, all of them named when it is blocked), so tiers don't multiply the trials below.
A step goes on a machine a crafting job can use (`planFor` passes `idle`: opted in, with power or a net that can run the
recipe), the fastest of those; only where none can does it fall back to a machine that is built, opted-in ones first, and
say what to switch on (`PlanStep.idle`, shown by the step and in the summary). Ways that would sit idle lose to other ways
of making the thing that need no more gathering. A guaranteed extra is still no way to make something (creosote, only ever beside coke). A recipe that `holds`
something asks for one as a tool, unless a machine built already holds it. Gatherable items are never made, only gathered. A hand tool a step needs is made
once if storage has none, and stays. The target is always made, not taken from storage. A machine recipe counts on a
built machine (any that `recipesForMachine` says can run it, the fastest); without one it is still planned, but
blocked, with the machine it needs, so steel without an EBF shows the whole chain and the one thing in the way. A
recipe cycle is blocked where it closes, never looped.

Where there are several ways to make something, it plans each in a trial (a child ledger that reads through to the
plan so far and keeps its own changes) and keeps the best: fewest blocked, then doable without gathering, then
cheapest (fewest items taken from storage or gathered, tools included), then quickest, then fewest hand crafts; ties
go to hand crafts before machines, then by recipe id, so the same storage always gives the same plan. The choice is
greedy, one need at a time. A whole EBF from nothing, down to cobblestone, with every machine built, plans in a few tens of milliseconds.

The planner doesn't model fuel and power (the coal furnace's coal, boilers' steam, EU), machines that are busy, or
running steps on several machines at once (`totals.ticks` is all machine time, one after another): a job deals with
those as it runs. Steps that make the same
thing in different places in the tree stay separate steps; `totals.byRecipe` adds them up.

**Autocrafting: the terminal.** The ME Terminal (`src/sim/data/me.ts`) is a 1 x 1 LV block, crafted at the workbench in
the LV tier (an LV hull, two circuits, glass for the screen, copper wire: a placeholder cost), so it comes once circuits
do. It sits on an EU net like any LV machine, and explodes above LV, but draws no power yet. Opening it shows the
terminal panel (`src/view/ui/terminal.ts`, through `openOverlay`) instead of its figure: the figure's screen is a fixed
sample, and the panel needs the width for the plan. `MachineDef.terminal` marks it.
- *Browse.* Everything in storage as a grid of icons and counts, by name. The search (above the redrawn part, as in the
  workbench) narrows it by item name and adds everything that can be made and storage has none of. Gathered things
  show but can't be picked: there is nothing to plan.
- *Plan.* Pick something and an amount (1, 8, 64, or typed, up to 10,000; the typed field keeps its focus as the panel
  redraws). `planFor(w, item, n)` plans from storage with the machines on every floor. The steps show as a tree, the
  target on top and what goes into each step under it, numbered in the order they run; each says what it makes, by
  hand or on which machine, its runs and time, what it uses (tools marked) and, when blocked, why. Below: what is taken
  from storage, what to gather, what is blocked and why, tools it makes, leftovers, byproducts and chances, machine time
  per machine and in total. Steps that rest on chance say so ("about 10 runs, on average"), and so does the summary.
  Everything that comes by chance can be picked; only cobblestone can't, being gathered. A line on top sums it up: ready, gather first, blocked, or machine runs waiting for jobs.
  The plan is cached until storage, the machines or the ask change (the panel redraws ten times a second).
- *Craft now.* When the plan is all hand crafts with nothing missing or blocked, Craft runs it: `craftNow(w, item, n)`
  plans again and runs each step through the workbench's `craft()`, in order, all or nothing (storage and stats go
  back if a step fails), with a sound and a toast. `whyNotNow(plan)` says why not otherwise.
- *Craft as a job.* A plan with machine steps starts as a crafting job instead (below), when nothing in it is blocked
  and a Crafting CPU is free (`whyNotJob`); it may have things to gather, and waits for them. The jobs under way are
  listed over the plan: each with its progress bar, time so far, why it is paused, a Cancel button, and every step
  with its runs done and where it stands (done, after which steps, on which machines, or what holds it up).

**Autocrafting: crafting jobs.** `src/sim/jobs.ts` carries a plan out over time, ticking with the world (after the
machines, so a machine that finished a run this tick can be handed back and lent again at once).
- *The Crafting CPU* (`src/sim/data/me.ts`, `MachineDef.cpu`) is a 1 x 1 LV block like the terminal (a placeholder
  workbench recipe: an LV hull, four circuits, copper wire), on an EU net but drawing nothing. Each CPU on any floor
  runs one job at a time, so more CPUs run more jobs at once. It is switched on when placed; switched off, it takes no
  job and its job pauses. A job whose CPU is removed moves to a free one, or pauses until there is one. Its figure runs
  (blades, fan) while it has a job, its meter is the job's progress, and it blinks busier while the job is going than
  while it is paused; its lamp on the floor is green while the job is going.
- *Machines opt in.* Every machine that runs recipes has an "Available for autocrafting" switch under its title in its
  GUI (figure or plain panel alike), saved as `autocraft`. Jobs borrow only opted-in machines, on any floor; a machine
  that isn't opted in keeps working for the player whatever a job needs. An opted-in machine is borrowed only when idle
  (no run under way), so one the player runs is taken between runs.
- *Steps.* `startJob(w, item, n)` plans afresh and copies the steps, each with `after`: the earlier steps that make
  something it uses (inputs or tools). A step goes when those are done, so independent branches run side by side, and a
  step never takes what an earlier step's user still needs (the plan's order guarantees enough once they are done).
  A hand step crafts as many runs as storage allows at once, and the rest when it can. A machine step lends its runs
  to the idle opted-in machines that can run its recipe (any machine of the right kind and power, not only the one the
  plan named) and are powered for it: an EU machine on a net with enough voltage for the recipe as that machine runs
  it, a steam machine on a net with steam or a lit boiler. The runs are split as evenly as they go across all of them.
- *Steps that rest on chance.* A step marked `average` in the plan isn't done after its runs, but when it has made what
  the plan needs out of it (`until`: each with `got`, counted as a machine's run finishes). It is lent a run at a time to
  every machine that can take it, and when the last find comes in, a run still going is given up and its inputs
  returned. If luck runs it out of input, or anything else takes what a step needs, it asks the step that made that for
  more (`makeMore`: more runs of a sure step, a higher goal for a chanced one), and waits for it; those ask the steps before them
  in turn, down to "3 more Cobblestone (gather it)". A job's progress counts an average step by what it has found. The
  terminal shows it as finds so far of finds wanted. A recipe that `holds` something only goes to a machine that has one
  or can take one from storage.
- *Moving tier.* A step runs on the tier the plan chose. If nothing there can take it any more (no machine opted in or with
  power) and no machine lent to it still has power, it moves to the quickest other tier that does the same work and has one
  (`moveTier`; `sameWork` says which recipes those are): runs done stay done, a run under way on a machine that lost its power gives
  its inputs back. It stays on its tier while that tier has a usable machine, even a busy one, so it isn't split onto a slower one.
- *A lease* (`MachineState.job`) sets the machine to the step's recipe, switched on, for exactly its runs: it starts no
  run beyond them. Each run counts towards its step as it finishes, so a machine removed mid-step keeps what it did
  (its run under way gives its inputs back, as any removal does) and the rest of its runs go back to the step. Then it
  is handed back with the recipe and switch the player left it with. A player who changes its recipe or switches it off
  takes it back: the lease ends there.
- *Pausing.* Each step says why its runs can't go out (`wait`): no machine of its kind is available for autocrafting,
  every one is busy, the idle one has no power or too low a voltage, or inputs are short ("2 more Iron Ore (gather
  it)"). A job with nothing going this tick is paused with the first step's reason, and goes on by itself when it can.
  Nothing is reserved: another job or the player can take what a job planned on, and the job waits for more.
- *Cancel* hands every borrowed machine back (a run under way is given up and its inputs returned) and drops the job;
  what it made stays in storage. A finished job makes a toast and a three-note chime.
- *Saves* are format 7: `jobs` (each step with `after`, runs done, its reason, and `until` if it rests on chance) and
  `nextJob`, a machine's `autocraft`, what it is `holding`, and a lent machine's lease. A lease whose job is gone (dropped
  on load because this version doesn't know one of its recipes or items) hands the machine back. Format 5 and older load
  with no jobs and no machine opted in.
